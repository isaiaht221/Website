Adoption of the Linux Ecosystem in the Public Sectors Across the European Union.

“With free software, users [...] control what their computers do”. On the other hand, “proprietary program[s] gives its developer power over its users” (Stallman). Free software is more than just the price tag. Free open-source software gives you the freedom to use and edit the software however you like. Linux is a free open-source operating system that has begun to see widespread adoption not only by home users but also large entities like corporations and governments. Some of the most notable examples of Linux adoption are countries in the European Union. From military to civil work, there are numerous examples of countries such as France and Germany adopting the Linux ecosystem in their public sectors.

The operating system determines more than just how your system looks. The operating system can cost money, determine eligible software, and massively affect security options. A good operating system allows secure and efficient usage. It is even more so important for government employees to be equipped with the right operating system for the job. A faulty operating system will be tedious to work with or leave massive security vulnerabilities. For decades, Windows have been trusted with government work, but now nations are questioning whether a private American owned operating system is the right tool for the job.

Countries switching their infrastructure and desktops to Linux in their public sectors benefit them by saving money, obtaining digital sovereignty and thus increasing national security. The move requires time to both develop and learn Linux based solutions, money to hire professionals to ease the transition, and requires the country to manage their own security. The path moving forward involves educating the workers and younger generation about Linux software and hire experts to transition over and maintain Linux.
