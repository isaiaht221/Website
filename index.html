Abstract

Linux has been growing in popularity rapidly. Most companies are already using Linux for their servers, and many home users are starting to switch. The European union has had some attempts and will no doubt have more at widespread adoption of the Linux ecosystem in their public sectors.

The adoption of Linux ecosystems can bring many great benefits and opportunities for the European Union’s public sectors and provides an essential framework for safeguarding their sovereignty. With the elimination of the vendor lock-in, a better and well-developed ecosystem comes into place. The EU would no longer have to rely on third party software-companies that aren’t based within, and there would no longer be constraints to what a business or government could do with the software. This ensures greater security but also ensures a beneficial and cost-effective path for the future.

The adoption of Linux brings about a few issues. The initial costs both of time and money are high. As seen in Munich, getting employees on board with Linux is difficult. Security is to be maintained by the European Union instead of being partly managed by Microsoft.

Audits, bug bounties, and working with companies in the Linux industry like red hat will keep security high. The government should slowly introduce the employees to software apart of the Linux ecosystem like Libre Office before fully transitioning to get employees used to the environment. Learning about Linux and the benefits of open source should be part of primary school leading to widespread adoption. Giving the freedom to choose a free operating system rather than reinforcing the duopoly Windows and MacOS have, reinforces the European Union’s values like freedom.
